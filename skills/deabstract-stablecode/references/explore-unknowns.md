# Explore material unknowns

Adapted from David Zhang's explore-unknowns; attribution and differences are in
[NOTICE](../NOTICE.md). This is a proportionate investigation, not a compulsory
multi-turn interview. Read the [entry contract](../SKILL.md) first.

Before making a consequential commitment, distinguish four kinds of information:
settled facts with evidence; known open questions; tacit expectations visible in
existing behavior or user preferences; and blind spots exposed by failure cases,
platform constraints or prior art. Existing answers need not be elicited again.

Read the actual repository and selected platform/library sources. Search for an
existing standard, algorithm, library or implementation before designing a custom
replacement. Record versions actually inspected. Label assumptions, inference and
synthetic examples; do not turn a plausible statement into an observed fact.

For an unresolved choice, show a small concrete example or comparison rather than
asking the user to imagine every detail. Investigate factual questions yourself.
Only genuinely unresolved product/authority choices need owner input. Do not hide
material findings until a scheduled stage or treat silence as approval.

A proof of concept needs a decision it could invalidate, the smallest discriminating
experiment, expected observations, cost/scope and a stop condition. Do not construct
a complete framework to test one architectural uncertainty. Stop expanding research
when the material decision is supported; do not claim all possible unknowns have
been exhausted.

Route decisions deliberately: a required capability, output or failure promise goes
to the specification; a language, runtime, data structure, dependency or architecture
goes to the plan. Correct that routing before acceptance. Record what the plan
leaves to local implementation discretion. A research note is evidence, not a new
authority document. Update the existing decision record and hand off unresolved
questions with their consequence and next discriminating observation.
